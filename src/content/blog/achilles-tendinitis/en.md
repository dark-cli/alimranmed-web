      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          An overuse injury of the Achilles tendon — the band of tissue connecting the calf muscles at the back of the
          lower leg to the heel bone.
      - label: Who gets it
        value: >-
          Runners who suddenly increase intensity or distance, and middle-aged people who play tennis, basketball or
          similar sports only on weekends.
      - label: Outlook
        value: >-
          Most cases settle with simple at-home care under a doctor's supervision. Severe cases can progress to a tendon
          rupture that may need surgical repair.

  - type: prose
    heading: Symptoms
    body: >
      The pain usually begins as a mild ache at the back of the leg or just above the heel after running or other sports
      activity. More severe episodes can follow prolonged running, stair climbing, or sprinting.


      Tenderness and stiffness are also common — most noticeable first thing in the morning, and typically easing with
      light activity.
  - type: prose
    heading: Causes
    body: >
      Achilles tendinitis is caused by repetitive or intense strain on the tendon, which is loaded every time you walk,
      run, jump, or push up onto your toes.


      The tendon's structure weakens with age, making it more vulnerable to injury — particularly in people who play
      sports only on weekends, or who suddenly step up the intensity of their training.
  - type: prose
    heading: Risk factors
    body: >
      A number of factors raise the risk of Achilles tendinitis:


      - **Sex.** It occurs most commonly in men.

      - **Age.** Incidence rises with age.

      - **Physical factors.** A naturally flat foot arch, obesity, and tight calf muscles all place extra strain on the
      tendon.

      - **Training choices.** Running in worn-out shoes, running in cold weather, and running on hilly terrain all
      increase the risk.

      - **Medical conditions.** People with psoriasis or high blood pressure are at higher risk.

      - **Medications.** A class of antibiotics called fluoroquinolones has been associated with higher rates of
      Achilles tendinitis.
  - type: prose
    heading: Complications
    body: >
      Chronic tendinitis weakens the Achilles tendon and makes it more vulnerable to a full tear (rupture) — a painful
      injury that usually requires surgical repair.
  - type: prose
    heading: Treatment
    body: >
      Tendinitis usually responds well to self-care measures. When the signs and symptoms are severe or persistent,
      further options are considered.


      If over-the-counter pain medications — such as ibuprofen or naproxen — are not enough, stronger anti-inflammatory
      medication may be prescribed to reduce inflammation and relieve pain.
