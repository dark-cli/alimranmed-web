  - type: prose
    heading: How it works
    body: >
      An intrathecal pump is a battery-operated device which delivers medication directly into the spinal canal. It may
      be used to treat chronic pain or spasticity, and is sometimes used for cancer-related pain.


      The spinal canal contains a large amount of fluid — cerebrospinal fluid, or CSF — in which the nerves bathe. By
      inserting a small catheter connected to a programmable reservoir, medications such as pain-killers can be
      delivered directly to these nerves.


      The reservoir (or chamber) is implanted under the skin of the abdomen or back, and can be refilled from time to
      time by inserting a small needle through the skin.
  - type: prose
    heading: Which conditions can be treated
    body: |
      An intrathecal pump is considered for a defined set of conditions:

      - **Chronic pain conditions**
        - [Complex regional pain syndrome (neuropathic pain)](/treatments/complex-regional-pain/)
        - [Multiple sclerosis pain](/treatments/multiple-sclerosis/)
        - Failed back surgery pain
      - **Cancer-related pain**
      - **Spasticity**
        - [Cerebral palsy](/treatments/cerebral-palsy/)
        - [Brain injury](/treatments/brain/)
  - type: prose
    heading: When surgery is considered
    body: >
      When medications and other treatments are no longer working well, or when their side effects have become too
      severe, surgery to implant a pump may be of value.


      A number of medications are useful for pain before this point is reached: standard opioid and non-opioid
      analgesics, anti-inflammatory medications, membrane-stabilising agents and anticonvulsants, as well as pregabalin.
      Special medical treatments such as ketamine infusions, local nerve and joint blocks, and other surgical options
      such as spinal cord stimulation should also be considered before moving to a pump.
  - type: prose
    heading: Aims and potential benefits
    body: >
      The goals of intrathecal medication delivery are:


      - Effective pain relief

      - Decreased spasticity

      - Reduced medication requirements and fewer systemic side effects (such as drowsiness)

      - Improved quality of life


      The chance of obtaining a significant benefit depends on a wide variety of factors. Your neurosurgeon will give
      you an indication of the likelihood of success in your specific case.


      If treatment is not undertaken, the likely outcomes are ongoing pain or spasticity and a reduced quality of life.
  - type: prose
    heading: How the procedure is performed
    body: >
      An intrathecal pump is inserted through a simple and safe surgical procedure. A small incision is made in the
      back, and a flexible catheter is introduced into the spinal canal. This is then connected to a battery-operated
      pump placed under the skin of the abdomen.


      Complications are uncommon, and may include bleeding and infection. The patient is discharged on either the same
      day or the day after surgery.
