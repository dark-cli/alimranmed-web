Poliomyelitis is a neurological disease caused by the poliovirus.

Fortunately, thanks to the systematic vaccination campaigns that began in 1963 in our country, the disease has been virtually eliminated since the late 1960s. The last case was documented in 1988. In recent years we have found new cases among immigrant populations or after travel to countries where the disease is still endemic.

Over the years, people with polio may develop movement disorders resulting from the neurological damage, such as scoliosis (curvature of the spine), tendon disorders in the back, or peripheral nerve entrapment, with the need for assistance to walk or the use of a wheelchair. Respiratory failure, osteoporotic fractures, and eventual loss of function and independence may also occur.

A small percentage of these people may develop what is called post-polio syndrome, which is the onset of new muscle weakness added to the previous weakness, without it being attributable to any other cause. This muscle weakness may or may not be accompanied by fatigue, muscle pain (myalgia), muscle atrophy and subsequent loss of function. If an electromyogram (EMG) is performed, we can confirm that this is truly post-polio syndrome (Halstead criteria).

In this disease, the best treatment option at present is preventing functional decline through follow-up by a skilled clinical specialist, together with surgery when indicated and good maintenance rehabilitation.

Treatment of polio:

Management focuses on preventing or minimizing deformities and maximizing the child’s capacity at home and in the community. The child is best treated with a multidisciplinary team that may include the following health care providers:

Nursing coordinator  
Neurosurgeon – a surgeon who specializes in operations on the brain and spinal cord.  
A neurologist – a doctor who specializes in conditions of the brain, spinal cord, and nerves.  
Physical and Occupational Therapist  
Orthopedic surgeon – a surgeon specializing in conditions of muscles, ligaments, tendons, and bones.  
Orthotist – a specialist in making braces and splints.
