Lower back and neck pain can be managed with traditional treatment options

such as physical therapy, chiropractic care or pain management, but disc pain is often

more difficult to treat. For many patients, treating the underlying disc disease is the single most effective way to solve the problem of that pain.

Through daily activities, spinal discs are prone to degeneration and injury because they are compressed and twisted.

When a disc degenerates, its gel-like nucleus loses its hydration, reducing the height of the disc.

Furthermore, disc degeneration causes the flexible outer shell to become fragile

and prone to cracks that can lead to disc herniation. Bulging or herniated discs

often press on the spinal nerves, causing severe pain and radicular symptoms.

®SpineMED is designed to target damaged spinal segments and decompress the spaces between the vertebrae and discs, in order to enhance the body's natural ability to heal without medication or surgery.

Damaged spinal discs rarely heal because they remain under constant pressure (even if the person feels comfortable). The ideal treatment for disc improvement is to relieve the pressure, or reduce the pressure inside the damaged disc.

How does SpineMED chiropractic work?

SpineMED® relieves pressure by stabilizing the spine without causing reflex muscle contractions or spasms, as happens with ordinary traction devices.

By monitoring the tension applied to the patient every 2.5 seconds, it can make adjustments every 20 seconds for better results.

This ability to sense and control tension almost instantly is a major difference that distinguishes it from other devices and traditional methods.

What is the point of decompression?  
The goal of decompression is to allow fluids, nutrients and oxygen to flow back into the disc. This nutrient exchange supports the body's natural rebuilding and can speed up the repair process, eventually healing damaged spinal discs.

In addition, reducing the pressure inside the disc may help draw the nucleus of a bulging or herniated disc back toward the center, relieving pressure on the nerve root and thereby easing the problem, pain or numbness.
