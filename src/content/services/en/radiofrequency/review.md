## Overview

Radiofrequency neurotomy is a minimally invasive procedure designed to alleviate chronic joint pain by applying heat to nerve pathways that transmit pain signals. Multiple clinical studies show that radiofrequency neurotomy significantly reduces pain severity for 1–2 years in most patients.

## Conditions Treated

The procedure addresses numerous chronic pain conditions, including carpal tunnel syndrome, knee pain, neck pain, trigeminal neuralgia, and osteoarthritis. The extensive list demonstrates the technique's versatility across musculoskeletal and neuropathic pain conditions.

## Key Benefits

- Pain relief lasting six months to two years
- Low complication and morbidity rates
- Greater range of motion and reduced medication dependency
- Quick recovery with minimal downtime

## Procedure Details

The technique uses local anesthesia and mild sedation rather than general anesthesia. A doctor guides a needle using X-ray or ultrasound imaging to the affected area, then delivers radiofrequency current to heat surrounding tissue.

## Safety Profile

RFA is generally well-tolerated, with very few associated complications. Minor risks include infection or bleeding at the insertion site.

## Recovery Restrictions

Patients should avoid driving for 24 hours, refrain from strenuous activities initially, and wait 1–2 days before bathing.
