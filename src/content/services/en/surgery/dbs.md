## Overview

Deep brain stimulation involves implanting electrodes within certain areas of your brain to regulate abnormal impulses or affect specific brain cells and chemicals. A pacemaker-like device placed under the skin controls the stimulation level, connected to the brain electrodes via a wire.

## Approved Conditions

The FDA has approved DBS treatment for:

- Dystonia
- Epilepsy
- Essential tremor
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder
- Parkinson's disease

## Research Applications

The procedure is currently being studied for potential treatment of addiction, chronic pain, cluster headaches, dementia, major depression, Huntington's disease, multiple sclerosis, stroke recovery, Tourette syndrome, and traumatic brain injury.

## Key Points About Results

Deep brain stimulation won't cure your disease, but it may help lessen your symptoms. While successful patients typically experience significant improvement, symptoms usually persist partially, and some conditions may still require medications. Success varies among patients, making pre-surgical consultation essential to set realistic expectations.

## Patient Eligibility

DBS is reserved for those who haven't achieved symptom control through medication alone.
