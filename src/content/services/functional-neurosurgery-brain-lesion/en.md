      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          A surgical technique that passes high-frequency radio waves through a brain probe to create a targeted lesion
          in specific brain tissue, performed under local anesthesia while the patient is awake.
      - label: What it treats
        value: >-
          Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, dystonia, and selected psychological disorders including obsessive-compulsive
          disorder, major depression, and refractory aggressive behaviour.
      - label: Approach
        value: >-
          MRI or CAT scan guidance with microelectrode recording to localise the target precisely. Several techniques
          exist — pallidotomy, thalamotomy, and subthalmotomy — chosen based on the specific condition and symptoms.
  - type: prose
    heading: What is radiofrequency brain lesioning surgery?
    body: >
      Radiofrequency brain lesioning surgery is primarily performed to treat Parkinson's disease, dystonia, tremor and
      psychological diseases. Radiofrequency is a type of high-frequency radio wave that can be used to create a lesion.
      The waves are passed through a probe in the brain that's used to target the correct tissue. It is done under local
      anesthesia (you aren't put to sleep), but because brain tissue feels no pain, the procedure is essentially painless.
      It is important for the patient to be awake during the procedure so that the surgeon can ask the patient a series of
      questions and perform a series of tests to localise the part of the brain causing the tremor or rigidity. These
      lesions target cells that control movement and are used to treat movement disorders, such as essential tremor,
      Parkinson's disease, and dystonia.
  - type: prose
    heading: Who might need this surgery?
    body: >
      You might have lesioning to treat Parkinson's disease if you have severe tremors (especially on one side), stiffness,
      or movement difficulties that are no longer controlled with medication, or serious motor problems from long-term use
      of levodopa (L-dopa).

      In those with essential tremor, propranolol and primidone are frontline treatments that reduce tremors for 50 to
      60 percent of people. Lesioning might be an option if these medications don't work, or the side effects are too severe.
  - type: prose
    heading: What to expect before and during surgery
    body: >
      Before surgery, your doctor will use an MRI or CAT scan to create a 3-D model of your brain. This allows them to see
      exactly what area of the brain to target. Your doctor might also use a procedure called microelectrode recording to
      detect how the cells in your brain fire. This helps them narrow down the target even more.
  - type: prose
    heading: Types of lesioning surgery
    body: >
      There are several different types of lesioning surgery. Those for Parkinson's disease and movement disorders include:

      - **Pallidotomy.** In this procedure, surgeons make a lesion in the Globus pallidus, a part of the brain that's
        overactive in Parkinson's disease. This surgery is not a good option for people with Parkinson's disease who don't
        respond well to L-dopa.

      - **Thalamotomy.** This procedure is mostly used for dystonia that affects one side of the body, or Parkinson's tremors
        on one side of the body. It targets the thalamus, a part of the brain that helps control motor responses.
        Thalamotomy is not used for bradykinesia, speech problems, or walking problems.

      - **Subthalmotomy.** This procedure targets the subthalamus, a small area of the brain that plays a role in motor
        control. It's the rarest type of lesioning.
  - type: prose
    heading: Lesioning for psychological disorders
    body: >
      In psychological disorders the lesion is usually made at bilateral sites. The most common targets include:

      - **Anterior Cingulotomy.** Posterior to anterior — most tip of frontal horn. Most commonly performed in North America
        for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). Also used in treatment of pain and major depression.

      - **Anterior Capsulotomy.** The target is the anterior limb of internal capsule at the border between putamen and
        pallidum, halfway between frontal horn tip and foramen of Munro. An effective and safe technique for the treatment
        of severe refractory OCD in patients who have no other alternative to improve their symptoms.

      - **Amygdalatomy.** Bilateral amygdala radio-frequency ablation effective for refractory aggressive behaviour that
        alters local cortical activity.
  - type: prose
    heading: Side effects of radiofrequency brain lesions
    body: >
      Common, but temporary, side effects of stereotactic thalamotomy include confusion, weakness, disturbed speech,
      balance problems, infection, and bleeding.
