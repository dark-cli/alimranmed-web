  - type: prose
    heading: How does pulsed shortwave therapy work?
    body: >
      Pulsed shortwave therapy (PSWT) involves a device that delivers an electric and magnetic field in short pulses
      with a time gap in between. Most of the published literature supports the therapeutic effects of the magnetic
      field. The evidence supports an effect similar to ultrasound therapy: an increase in activity at the cell
      membrane, which results in an up-regulated state in which materials are transported at a high rate in and out of
      the cell.


      A cell's contents determine its health, and the normal flow of ions is determined by a difference in voltage
      called the potential difference. During inflammation or other trauma, this potential is disturbed and flow is
      disrupted, damaging the cell and limiting tissue repair.


      The literature supports the claim that PSWT helps restore normal values and improve cell function by facilitating
      the normal transport of ions across the membrane. The process is thought to be selective: only damaged cells
      respond to the low levels of energy involved.
  - type: prose
    heading: The use of pulsed shortwave in physiotherapy
    body: >
      Each electrotherapy modality has its own pros, cons and specific uses. Ultrasound is effective in denser tissues
      with high concentrations of collagen, such as ligaments and tendons, while pulsed shortwave, because of its mode
      of action, is more useful in cells where ionic concentrations are vital to repair, namely muscle and nerve cells.


      Observed effects include an increase in healing structures such as white blood cells and fibroblasts around
      damaged cells, and a distinct reduction in inflammation. Physiotherapists use pulsed shortwave to treat muscle
      damage, particularly to reduce inflammation and promote the repair of strains and similar muscular problems. It
      can also be used to treat fractures and nerve damage.
  - type: prose
    heading: Contraindications
    body: >
      The electric and magnetic field used in this treatment makes it unsuitable for people with pacemakers and other
      implants, and for pregnant women because of potential effects on the fetus. Malignant tissue can proliferate
      faster under this therapy, so it is not suitable for cancer patients.
