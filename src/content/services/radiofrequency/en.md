Nerve ablation by radiofrequency waves (also called radiofrequency ablation or lesioning) is a minimally invasive procedure that can provide lasting relief for those suffering from facet joint pain. In fact, several clinical studies show that nerve ablation by radiofrequency waves significantly reduces pain intensity and frequency for 1 to 2 years in the majority of patients. Nerve ablation by radiofrequency waves involves applying heat to certain nerve pathways to "stop" the transmission of pain signals to the brain. It is performed on an outpatient basis and requires only local anesthesia and light sedation, which reduces the potential complications of open surgery and general anesthesia. It has a high success rate and low complication rate.

## **Which Conditions are Treated with Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

Radiofrequency nerve ablation can be used to help patients suffering from chronic lower back and neck pain and pain associated with joint degeneration from arthritis.

## **What are the Benefits of Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

-   Pain relief for up to two years.
-   Longer-lasting pain relief and fewer side effects compared to steroid injections.
-   Low rates of complications and morbidity.
-   Significant pain relief compared to surgery: nearly more than half of people suffering from back pain were not helped by surgery.
-   Greater range of motion.
-   Reduced use of pain medications.
-   Improved quality of life.
-   Short recovery time.

## **How Many Types of Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation are There?**

There are two types:

-   Thermal radiofrequency where tissue temperature reaches 80 degrees Celsius.
-   Pulsed radiofrequency where tissue temperature does not exceed 45 degrees Celsius.

## **What is the Duration of Pain Relief from Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

The degree of pain relief varies, depending on the cause and location of the pain. Pain relief from radiofrequency nerve ablation can last from six months to two years and in some cases pain relief can last for years. More than 70% of patients treated with radiofrequency nerve ablation experience pain relief.

## **Is Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation Safe?**

Radiofrequency nerve ablation has been proven to be a safe and effective method for treating certain forms of pain. It is also generally well tolerated, with very few complications associated with it. There is a slight risk of infection and bleeding at the insertion site. Your doctor can advise you on your specific risks.

## **Who Should Not Get Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

As with any medical procedure, radiofrequency nerve ablation is not suitable for everyone. For example, radiofrequency nerve ablation is not recommended for people who have active infections or blood clotting problems. Your doctor can tell you if you should not get radiofrequency nerve ablation.

## **How Do I Prepare for Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

To prepare for radiofrequency nerve ablation treatment, you should take some precautions, including:

-   Do not eat within six hours of your appointment; however, you may have clear fluids until two hours before the procedure.
-   If you have diabetes and use insulin, you should adjust your insulin dose on the day of the procedure. Your primary care doctor will help you with this adjustment. Bring your diabetes medication with you so you can take it after the procedure.
-   Continue taking all other medications with a small sip of water. Bring all medications with you so you can take them after the procedure. Please note: Do not stop taking any medication without consulting your primary care doctor or referring doctor first.
-   You will need to bring someone with you to drive you home after the procedure. You should not drive or operate machinery for at least 24 hours after the procedure.

## **What Happens During Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

You will meet with a doctor for evaluation. If radiofrequency nerve ablation is recommended, the doctor will explain the procedure to you in detail, including potential complications and side effects.

The doctor will also answer any questions you may have.

An IV line may be placed in a vein in your arm before the procedure and local anesthesia and light sedation may be used to reduce any discomfort during radiofrequency nerve ablation. You may be awake during the procedure to help properly assess the procedure. Ask your doctor for details beforehand.

After local anesthesia (you will be awake but will not feel any pain), the doctor will insert a small needle in the general area where you feel pain. Using X-rays or ultrasound, your doctor will guide the needle to the precise target area. A small electrode is then inserted through the needle to begin the stimulation process.

During the procedure, your doctor will ask if you are able to feel a tingling sensation. The purpose of the stimulation process is to help the doctor determine if the electrode is in the optimal area for treatment.

Once the position of the needle and electrode is verified, a small electrical current is sent through the electrode to the surrounding tissue, causing the tissue to heat up. You should not feel any discomfort during the heating part of the procedure.

## **What Happens After Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

After radiofrequency nerve ablation:

-   You will remain in the recovery room for monitoring, where a nurse will check your blood pressure and pulse.
-   A bandage will be placed on the injection site.
-   The nurse will give you a beverage and will review discharge instructions with you.
-   You should have someone drive you home.

## **Can I Resume My Normal Activities After Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

You will have some restrictions immediately after radiofrequency nerve ablation:

-   Do not drive or operate machinery for at least 24 hours after the procedure.
-   You may resume your normal diet.
-   Do not engage in any strenuous activity in the first 24 hours after the procedure.
-   Do not take a bath for one to two days after the procedure; you can shower.
-   You may remove any bandages in the evening before bed.

## **What Side Effects May I Experience After Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation?**

You may experience the following effects after radiofrequency nerve ablation:

-   **Numbness in the leg:** If you experience any numbness in the leg, walk only with assistance. This should last only a few hours and is due to the local anesthesia given during the procedure.
-   **Mild back discomfort:** This may occur when the local anesthesia wears off and usually lasts one to three days. Apply ice to the area on the day of the procedure and moist heat the next day if discomfort persists. You can also use your regular pain relievers.

## **Radiofrequency Nerve Ablation Warning**

If you experience sharp pain at the injection site, notice swelling and redness, or increasing weakness in the leg. You should have your doctor evaluate you for bleeding and injection complications.
