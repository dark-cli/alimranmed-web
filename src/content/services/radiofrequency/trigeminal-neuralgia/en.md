  - type: prose
    heading: Radiofrequency for trigeminal neuralgia at Alimran Medical Center
    body: >
      The Gasserian ganglion — the main sensory ganglion of the trigeminal nerve — sits in Meckel's Cave at the base of
      the skull. A partial radiofrequency ablation of this ganglion can effectively interrupt the pain pathway
      responsible for trigeminal neuralgia.


      The procedure is performed under local anaesthesia. A small, thin needle is inserted through the middle of the
      cheek and gently guided with X-ray assistance through the foramen ovale into Meckel's Cave where the ganglion
      sits. Before the ablation is performed, electrical stimulation is used to confirm precisely which branch of the
      trigeminal nerve is involved in the pain. This important step ensures that only the affected branch is treated,
      avoiding excessive post-procedure numbness.


      For patients where surgery and general anaesthesia are too risky — due to advanced age or medical comorbidities —
      this injection technique offers effective and durable pain relief with a favourable safety profile.
