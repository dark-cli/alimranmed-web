  - type: prose
    heading: "What brain tumour means"
    body: |
      A brain tumour is an abnormal mass of cells inside or immediately around the brain. Not every tumour is cancer, and not every cancer that reaches the brain is dangerous in the same way. The critical distinctions are: is it primary or secondary; is it benign or malignant; and — most importantly clinically — where is it and what is it pressing on.

      Two tumours of the same histology can have very different management if one is in silent brain territory and the other is next to the motor strip or speech area. The plan is made together with the patient after the imaging is reviewed in detail.

    facts:
      - "New-onset seizure in an adult without prior epilepsy warrants an MRI — a tumour is one of the things to rule out."
      - "Headache alone is rarely a brain tumour; the concerning pattern is early-morning headache with vomiting, or a change in an existing headache."
      - "The gold-standard investigation is MRI with contrast, not CT — CT is used only when speed matters (trauma, suspected haemorrhage)."
      - "Awake craniotomy allows resection close to eloquent brain areas while continuously monitoring speech and movement."
      - "Follow-up MRI at 3 and 6 months is standard after any resection — the interval scan matters as much as the operation."
