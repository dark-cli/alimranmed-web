  - type: highlights
    items:
      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          Severe pain in cancer patients caused by the tumour itself (invasion, compression, inflammation) or treatment
          side effects (surgery, chemotherapy, radiation). Intractable pain fails to respond to standard therapies.
      - label: Typical signs
        value: >-
          Persistent, often progressive pain; may be localised (surgical scar, nerve compression) or diffuse (bone
          metastases); often accompanied by depression, sleep disturbance and reduced quality of life.
      - label: How we treat it
        value: >-
          Escalate opioids according to WHO ladder. Add adjuvant medications (anticonvulsants, antidepressants).
          Interventional pain procedures (blocks, neurolysis, spinal infusions). Palliative care team for holistic
          support.
  - type: prose
    heading: What cancer pain is
    body: >
      Cancer-related pain affects 30–50% of patients during active treatment and up to 75% of those with advanced
      disease. Pain arises from tumour invasion of bone, viscera or nerves; treatment toxicity; infection; or
      post-treatment complications. Intractable cancer pain is severe enough to substantially impair function and
      quality of life despite conventional analgesics.
  - type: stats
    heading: What we see in clinic
    items:
      - value: 30–50%
        label: Of cancer patients experience pain during active treatment
      - value: 75%
        label: With advanced disease have pain
      - value: 60–70%
        label: Achieve adequate analgesia with opioids and adjuvants; 30% require interventional procedures for full control
  - type: facts
    items:
      - Uncontrolled cancer pain is one of the strongest predictors of desire for hastened death or suicide.
      - Tolerance (the need for escalating doses) develops over months to years; this is different from addiction.
      - >-
        Interventional pain procedures (nerve blocks, spinal infusions) can dramatically reduce opioid requirements and
        side effects.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: >-
      WHO analgesic ladder guides escalation. Multimodal therapy (opioids + adjuvants + interventional procedures) is
      standard for intractable pain.
    note: >-
      Palliative care is NOT end-of-life care — it is concurrent with curative treatment and focuses on symptom
      management and quality of life.
    panels:
      - title: Pharmacotherapy — WHO ladder
        subtitle: "Step 1: mild pain (1–3/10)"
        items:
          - NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen)
          - Paracetamol
          - "Consider adjuvants: anticonvulsants, antidepressants"
      - title: Pharmacotherapy — WHO ladder
        subtitle: "Step 2: moderate pain (4–6/10)"
        items:
          - Weak opioids (codeine, tramadol) ± NSAIDs
          - Adjuvant medications as above
      - title: Pharmacotherapy — WHO ladder
        subtitle: "Step 3: severe pain (7–10/10)"
        items:
          - Strong opioids (morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone) — no ceiling dose
          - Extended-release formulations for background pain
          - Short-acting (breakthrough) formulations available
          - "Adjuvants: gabapentin, pregabalin, tricyclics, steroids"
      - title: Interventional pain procedures
        subtitle: Intractable pain despite maximal pharmacotherapy
        items:
          - Regional nerve blocks (peripheral and plexus blocks)
          - Spinal/epidural infusions (continuous opioid ± local anaesthetic)
          - Neurolytic procedures (alcohol or phenol injection) for life-threatening pain in terminal illness
          - Dorsal root entry zone (DREZ) lesioning for neuropathic cancer pain
