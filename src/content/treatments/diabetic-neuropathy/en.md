  - type: prose
    heading: What diabetic neuropathy is
    body: >
      Diabetic neuropathy is nerve damage caused by prolonged high blood glucose. High sugar levels impair nerves'
      ability to transmit signals and damage the small blood vessels that supply oxygen and nutrients to the nerves.
      About 50% of people with diabetes develop some form of neuropathy; risk increases the longer diabetes is present.


      **There are four main types:**


      **1. Peripheral neuropathy** (most common) — affects the feet and legs first, then the arms and hands. Symptoms
      worsen at night and include: numbness or reduced pain/temperature sensation, tingling, burning, sharp pains or
      cramps, extreme touch sensitivity, and foot ulcers or infections.


      **2. Autonomic neuropathy** — affects nerves controlling internal organs (heart, bladder, digestive system, sexual
      organs). Can cause: bladder dysfunction, gastroparesis (slow stomach emptying), digestive problems, sexual
      dysfunction, blood pressure drops on standing, and impaired hypoglycemia awareness.


      **3. Proximal neuropathy (diabetic amyotrophy)** — relatively rare (~1% of type 2 diabetes), affects older adults.
      Causes severe one-sided pain in the upper thigh, hip, or buttock, with thigh muscle weakness and sometimes
      significant weight loss.


      **4. Focal neuropathy (mononeuropathy)** — affects a single nerve, causing double vision, eye pain, facial
      paralysis (Bell's palsy), chest pain mimicking heart attack, or hand weakness.


      **Complications** of diabetic neuropathy include: foot ulcers and amputation risk, urinary tract infections and
      incontinence, sudden blood pressure drops, digestive problems, and sexual dysfunction.
