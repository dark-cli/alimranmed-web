  - type: prose
    heading: "What failed back surgery syndrome is"
    body: |
      Failed back surgery syndrome (FBSS) describes new or persistent pain after spinal surgery for back or leg pain. Pain may remain reduced but still present, or worsen within months of surgery — commonly from scar tissue forming around spinal nerve roots, alongside persistent tissue pain and muscle spasm.

      The term does not imply that anything went wrong during surgery. Even with the best surgeon and appropriate indications, spinal surgery predicts a successful outcome in no more than 95% of cases.

      **What spine surgery can achieve:**
      1. Decompressing a pinched nerve root
      2. Stabilising a painful joint

      Surgery cannot eliminate pain directly — it changes anatomy. For this reason, the anatomical lesion responsible for the pain must be accurately identified before surgery, not after. Repeated surgery carries a higher likelihood of FBSS.

      **Treatment pathway:** Initial treatment typically includes targeted injections, nerve blocks, or radiofrequency ablation to temporarily interrupt pain signals. A graduated exercise programme to restore muscle strength is also a core component.

      For pain that is disabling and does not respond to initial measures, **spinal cord stimulation** is an option. One or more leads with small electrical contacts are placed near the affected nerves (along the spine); if a trial period reduces pain, a small battery is implanted to provide continuous stimulation. Large, carefully conducted trials have shown spinal cord stimulation to achieve superior outcomes at lower cost than repeat back surgery for FBSS.
