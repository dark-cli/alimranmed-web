  - type: prose
    heading: What fibromyalgia is
    body: >
      Fibromyalgia is a complex condition characterised by widespread muscle pain. Some researchers believe it increases
      the body's pain sensations by affecting the way the brain processes pain signals — the pain receptors remember the
      signal, and eventually overreact to it.


      Muscle makes up most of the human body and plays an active role in movement, balance and coordination. Myofascial
      pain results from stress or damage to a muscle or its fascia, and is often overlooked despite being a major cause
      of pain and dysfunction.
  - type: quote
    text: >-
      Muscle and fascia are among the parts of the body most exposed to wear and damage. Yet clinicians tend to focus on
      bones, discs and nerves. That bias needs to change.
    attribution: Hussein Imran Mousa, consultant neurosurgeon
  - type: prose
    body: >
      In practice this means a patient can carry pain for years while imaging is repeatedly reported as normal. The
      diagnosis is made by history and physical examination of the muscles, not by the scanner.
  - type: panels
    heading: Trigger points
    intro: >-
      A myofascial trigger point is a small area of irritation in a muscle or its fascia that produces pain. Every
      trigger point is either active or latent, and the distinction changes how the patient experiences it.
    panels:
      - eyebrow: Type A
        title: Active trigger point
        items:
          - Produces pain at rest, and again on any movement that stretches the muscle.
          - Always tender; felt as a taut band between muscle fibres.
          - Often refers pain to a distant site — the source is not where the pain is felt.
      - eyebrow: Type B
        title: Latent trigger point
        items:
          - Silent at rest; only produces pain when pressed or overloaded.
          - Still restricts range of motion and reduces muscle strength.
          - Can become active under stress, fatigue, or a minor injury.

  - type: facts
    items:
      - Most patients present between 30–50 years old; women more often than men.
      - Diagnosis is clinical — imaging is usually unremarkable and rarely changes management.
      - Sleep disturbance and stress amplify the pain; addressing them is part of treatment, not optional.
      - Trigger points are palpable — a thorough muscle exam is the diagnostic tool, not the scanner.
      - Pain often refers away from its source; treating the referred site alone rarely works.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: >-
      Care is multimodal — no single intervention resolves fibromyalgia on its own. The clinic combines interventions
      from all four groups below, weighted to the individual patient's presentation.
    note: >-
      The actual regime varies from patient to patient. Selection is discussed at the first consultation after the
      muscle examination and imaging review.
    panels:
      - title: Injection therapy
        subtitle: Direct treatment of trigger points
        items:
          - Trigger point injection (local anaesthetic ± steroid)
          - Botulinum toxin for refractory muscle spasm
          - Sympathetic nerve blocks
          - Peripheral nerve blocks
      - title: Physiotherapy
        subtitle: Manual and movement therapy
        items:
          - Myofascial release
          - Stretching and postural work
          - Graded exercise therapy
          - Dry needling
      - title: Medication
        subtitle: Systemic support, not first line
        items:
          - Low-dose tricyclic antidepressants for sleep and pain modulation
          - SNRIs for centralised pain
          - Anticonvulsants where nerve pain dominates
      - title: Lifestyle
        subtitle: Non-negotiable adjuncts
        items:
          - Sleep hygiene review
          - Stress reduction (CBT, mindfulness)
          - Aerobic conditioning built up gradually
          - Weight management where relevant
