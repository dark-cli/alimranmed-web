      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          Knee pain from mechanical dysfunction (meniscal tear, ligament injury), osteoarthritis, or patellofemoral
          syndrome. Pain may be activity-related or constant.
      - label: Typical signs
        value: >-
          Pain with stairs, squatting, or prolonged standing, swelling, stiffness (worse in morning or after rest),
          giving way or locking sensations.
      - label: How we treat it
        value: >-
          Activity modification, physiotherapy, analgesia. Intra-articular corticosteroid or hyaluronic acid injection
          for arthritis. MRI if meniscal or ligament tear suspected. Surgery (arthroscopy, reconstruction) if
          conservative therapy fails.
  - type: prose
    heading: What knee pain is
    body: >
      Knee pain is extremely common, affecting 25% of adults. Causes include osteoarthritis (the most common), meniscal
      tears, ligament injuries (ACL, MCL), and patellofemoral syndrome. Mechanical dysfunction from weak hip or
      quadriceps muscles is a frequent contributor.
  - type: stats
    heading: What we see in clinic
    items:
      - value: 25%
        label: Of adults report knee pain
      - value: 80%
        label: With mild–moderate OA respond to physiotherapy and weight management
      - value: 60–70%
        label: Achieve pain relief and functional improvement after intra-articular injection
  - type: facts
    items:
      - >-
        MRI findings (meniscal tears, cartilage loss) are common in asymptomatic individuals — findings must correlate
        with symptoms.
      - Physiotherapy addressing hip and quadriceps strength is more effective than bracing alone.
      - >-
        Intra-articular corticosteroid injections provide 6–12 weeks of relief; hyaluronic acid injections may have
        longer-lasting benefit.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: >-
      Early intervention with physiotherapy and activity modification prevents progression. Intra-articular injections
      and surgery are reserved for refractory cases.
    panels:
      - title: Activity modification
        subtitle: All patients
        items:
          - Avoid high-impact activities (running, jumping) temporarily
          - Maintain low-impact activities (walking, swimming, cycling)
          - Weight loss (even 5% improves symptoms significantly)
      - title: Physiotherapy
        subtitle: First-line for all mechanical knee pain
        items:
          - Quadriceps strengthening (crucial for patellar tracking)
          - Hip abductor and external rotator strengthening
          - Flexibility and gait retraining
      - title: Intra-articular injection
        subtitle: Osteoarthritis or post-injury inflammation
        items:
          - Corticosteroid injection — fast-acting, lasts 6–12 weeks
          - Hyaluronic acid injection — slower onset, may last 6–12 months
          - Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injection — emerging option for cartilage preservation
      - title: Surgery
        subtitle: Mechanical block or significant structural damage
        items:
          - Arthroscopic meniscectomy for mechanical meniscal tears
          - ACL reconstruction if giving-way episodes are disabling
          - Osteotomy or arthroplasty for advanced osteoarthritis
