  - type: prose
    heading: What a meniscal tear is
    body: >
      The menisci are crescent-shaped cartilage structures in the knee joint. There are two menisci — medial (internal)
      and lateral (external) — situated between the tibia and the femur. Composed of dense collagen fibres, the menisci
      resist compression and provide elasticity. They absorb shocks, distribute load over a wider surface area, protect
      the articular cartilage from excessive pressure, and contribute to knee stability.
  - type: prose
    heading: How a meniscus tear occurs
    body: >
      Meniscus tears can occur at any age, though they are frequently seen in athletes and are often considered a sports
      injury. Most injuries happen during sudden twisting or rotational movements.


      In young individuals, meniscal tissue is robust, so significant trauma is required to produce a tear. In older
      adults, degenerative changes weaken the meniscus, making tears possible even with mild trauma.
  - type: prose
    heading: Symptoms of a meniscal tear
    body: >
      Pain is the primary clinical finding. It typically occurs during knee flexion. Depending on tear size, patients may
      also report locking or catching sensations in the knee. Because the meniscus is compressed during stair climbing
      and squatting, pain increases in these positions and locking may occur when the knee is bent.


      Joint effusion is common — patients describe a sense of swelling or fullness in the knee. Early diagnosis is
      important; prolonged meniscal tearing can lead to knee instability, cartilage damage, and osteoarthritis.
  - type: prose
    heading: Physical therapy for meniscus tears
    body: >
      Physiotherapy is used as conservative treatment for incomplete meniscus tears. The goals are to eliminate pain,
      swelling and joint restriction, and to strengthen the muscles around the knee.


      Physiotherapy plays a critical role in oedema control, prevention of muscle atrophy, pain management, and returning
      the patient to normal daily activities.
  - type: prose
    heading: Post-surgical physiotherapy
    body: >
      Advanced meniscus tears are now treated surgically. Depending on the tear's location and characteristics, the
      procedure is either meniscal repair or meniscectomy (removal of the torn meniscus).


      After surgery, physiotherapy is essential. The duration varies from 2 weeks to 6 weeks depending on the surgical
      technique. The main goals are reducing pain and inflammation, increasing muscle strength and endurance, and helping
      the joint regain function — since the articular cartilage must adapt to functioning without meniscal protection.


      Physical therapy modalities and appropriate exercise programmes are used for this purpose. Exercises are progressed
      gradually in the postoperative period and are tailored to the patient's condition; they should not be performed
      without professional guidance.
