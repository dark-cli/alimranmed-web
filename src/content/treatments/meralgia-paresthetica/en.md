  - type: prose
    heading: "What meralgia paresthetica is"
    body: |
      Meralgia paresthetica occurs when the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve — a pure sensory nerve with no motor function — is compressed or entrapped. This nerve branches from the lumbar plexus, passes through the pelvis and groin via the inguinal ligament, and provides sensation to the front and outer surface of the thigh.

      When compressed, it produces burning, tingling, or numbness along the outer thigh, often on one side. Symptoms typically worsen after prolonged standing or walking. Because the nerve is sensory only, muscle weakness is not a feature.

      **Common causes:**
      - Tight clothing such as belts, corsets, or tight trousers
      - Obesity or significant weight gain
      - Pregnancy
      - Hip injury
      - Repetitive leg movements
      - Scar tissue near the inguinal ligament from prior injury or surgery

      In many cases symptoms improve within a few months. Conservative measures — wearing loose clothing and managing weight — are tried first. When these are not enough, targeted treatments are available.
