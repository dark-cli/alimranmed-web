  - type: highlights
    items:
      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          Group of conditions with abnormal muscle tone or involuntary movement — cerebral palsy, spasticity, dystonia,
          ataxia. May be congenital, acquired, or progressive.
      - label: Typical signs
        value: >-
          Stiffness, involuntary writhing or tremor, difficulty initiating movement, imbalance, or uncontrolled limb
          movements.
      - label: How we treat it
        value: >-
          Physical therapy and stretching are foundation. Botulinum toxin for focal spasticity. Functional neurosurgery
          (selective dorsal rhizotomy, stereotactic lesioning) for severe, disabling cases.
  - type: prose
    heading: What motor disorders are
    body: >
      Motor disorders span a spectrum from congenital cerebral palsy to acquired spasticity following stroke, spinal
      cord injury or brain trauma. The underlying problem is loss of normal inhibitory control over motor neurons,
      resulting in overactive reflexes, muscle tightness or involuntary movement.
  - type: stats
    heading: What we see in clinic
    items:
      - value: 2–3 per 1000
        label: Children born with cerebral palsy
      - value: 30%
        label: Of stroke survivors develop spasticity within weeks
      - value: 60–80%
        label: Achieve functional improvement with botulinum toxin
  - type: facts
    items:
      - Spasticity is velocity-dependent stiffness — resistance increases with speed of stretch.
      - MRI or CT needed to rule out progressive structural lesions.
      - Botulinum toxin works within 1–2 weeks and lasts 3–4 months, requiring repeat injections.
      - >-
        Functional neurosurgery (dorsal rhizotomy, GPi deep brain stimulation) is reserved for severe cases refractory
        to conservative management.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: Physical therapy is the foundation. Pharmacological and surgical options escalate with severity.
    panels:
      - title: Physical therapy
        subtitle: First-line for all motor disorders
        items:
          - Stretching and positioning to prevent contracture
          - Gait training and balance exercises
          - Constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT)
      - title: Botulinum toxin
        subtitle: Focal spasticity not responsive to therapy alone
        items:
          - Injection into overactive muscles
          - Blocks acetylcholine release, weakening muscle
          - Effect starts in 1–2 weeks, lasts 3–4 months
      - title: Oral medication
        subtitle: Generalised spasticity
        items:
          - Baclofen (GABA agonist)
          - Tizanidine (alpha-2 agonist)
          - Dantrolene (direct muscle relaxant)
      - title: Functional neurosurgery
        subtitle: Severe, disabling cases refractory to conservative therapy
        items:
          - Selective dorsal rhizotomy (SDR) in children with cerebral palsy
          - Stereotactic lesioning (pallidotomy, thalamotomy) for dystonia
          - Deep brain stimulation (GPi) for generalised dystonia
