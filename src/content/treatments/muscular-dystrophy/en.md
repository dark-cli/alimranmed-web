  - type: prose
    heading: What muscular dystrophy is
    body: >
      Muscular dystrophy (MD) is the name given to a group of diseases that cause
      progressive weakness and loss of muscle mass. Muscle diseases often occur
      at an early age. Although congenital muscular dystrophies start to show
      symptoms from birth, symptoms in other diseases of this group usually begin
      after the age of three.


      Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is the most common congenital muscle
      disease. Since the disease is transmitted by the X chromosome, the majority
      of patients are men. The incidence of this disease, which is 1 in 3,500 in
      men, is expressed in figures of 1 in a million in women. More than 30
      muscular dystrophy diseases have been described today. These diseases
      differ according to the genes that cause disease, the affected muscles, the
      age at which symptoms first appear, and the progression age of the disease,
      and are classified accordingly.
  - type: prose
    heading: Causes
    body: >
      Muscular dystrophies can be genetic or can occur as a result of changes in
      genes. In Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophies, there is a deficiency
      in the synthesis of a protein called dystrophin, which is genetically
      responsible for muscle nutrition. In this disease, since the dystrophin
      gene is carried on the X chromosome, it is X-linked and is very common in
      males.
  - type: prose
    heading: Symptoms and progression
    body: >
      Most of the symptoms of congenital muscle diseases occur in childhood or
      adolescence. Congenital muscular dystrophies only start from birth or
      within the first 2 months and can be seen in both sexes. General symptoms
      include:

      - Walking on toes

      - Having weak muscles, falling frequently

      - Recurrence of muscle cramps

      - Difficulty getting up, climbing stairs, running and jumping

      - Often having a curved spine (scoliosis)

      Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), which is the most common among muscular
      dystrophies, begins to show symptoms at the age of 3–5. Sometimes it may
      occur at an earlier age with symptoms such as late start to walk, laxity in
      muscles and falling while walking. Generally, the first finding is the
      inability to straighten the head from the lying position due to the weakness
      of the neck muscles. Clumsiness, falling, and inability to play games with
      peers occur. This situation worries the family. A duck-like, rocking gait
      occurs as a result of the involvement of the shoulder and hip muscles.
      Therefore, the first symptoms are difficulties and frequent falls,
      especially when getting up from the ground, climbing stairs, or climbing
      uphill. Children can often stand up with their hands resting on the ground.

      Most of the other congenital muscle diseases, except DMD which occurs
      early, start at the age of 10–20. DMD progresses slowly (by creating a
      decrease in muscle strength) in children and can reach levels that can make
      them need a wheelchair at the age of 7–12.

      These people may need assistance in moving their arms, legs and bodies as
      a teenager. Most of the patients die in their 20s due to respiratory or
      cardiac reasons. The muscle breakdown in DMD does not cause any pain in the
      patient.
  - type: prose
    heading: Diagnosis
    body: >
      The frequent falls of children cause families to consult a doctor.
      Diagnostic tools include:

      - Blood test; CK is very high, genetic tests are applied

      - Muscle biopsy; shows changes in the muscles

      - EMG; indicates disturbances in muscle conduction and contraction

      - Muscle strength and reflex tests help the diagnosis
  - type: prose
    heading: Treatment and rehabilitation
    body: >
      There is no treatment method for DMD yet. Steroid drugs, some devices and
      physical therapy can be tried to extend the range of motion in patients.

      Supportive methods such as occupational therapy, speech therapy and
      pulmonary rehabilitation can be used in addition to physical therapy.
      Exercise should be done at a level that does not create fatigue. In order
      not to make children overweight, it is necessary to stay away from sugary,
      flour foods and beverages such as cola.

      In the treatment, it should be aimed to give the child a happy childhood.

      In DMD patients, a special sling should be worn, including the ankles, in
      order to prevent the ankles from looking downward, especially when lying
      down.

      Mothers known to be carriers of DMD should undergo genetic testing. As a
      result of genetic tests in new pregnancies, it will be understood to a
      great extent whether the fetus has DMD.
