      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          The subspecialty of neurosurgery focused on conditions affecting the developing brain, spinal cord, and
          nervous system in children. The paediatric nervous system is anatomically and physiologically distinct from
          the adult — the skull is compliant, the brain is rapidly growing, and many conditions are congenital or
          developmental rather than acquired. Surgical timing, technique, and approach must account for these
          differences.
      - label: Conditions treated
        value: >-
          Hydrocephalus (communicating and obstructive); tethered spinal cord syndrome; craniosynostosis (premature
          skull suture fusion); cerebral palsy (including spasticity management with intrathecal baclofen pump and
          selective dorsal rhizotomy); brain and spinal cord tumours; spina bifida and myelomeningocele; vascular
          malformations; epilepsy surgery; and peripheral nerve conditions in children.
      - label: Approach
        value: >-
          Early diagnosis is critical — the developing brain has greater plasticity and capacity for recovery when
          treated promptly, but also greater vulnerability to injury from untreated raised intracranial pressure or cord
          tethering. Multi-disciplinary management integrating paediatric neurology, neurosurgery, developmental
          paediatrics, physiotherapy, and ophthalmology. Most operative decisions balance timing against surgical risk
          in a growing child.
  - type: prose
    heading: Pediatric neurosurgery at Alimran Medical Center
    body: >
      Children's neurosurgical conditions are not simply smaller versions of adult disease — the developing nervous
      system has distinct anatomy, physiology, and vulnerability. The skull of a newborn is pliable and expandable;
      sutures are open; the brain consumes a disproportionately large fraction of cardiac output and is highly sensitive
      to changes in perfusion and oxygenation. Many conditions treated in paediatric neurosurgery are congenital or
      developmental in origin, present at or shortly after birth, and require different surgical approaches than
      equivalent pathology in adults.


      **Key conditions**


      *[Paediatric hydrocephalus](/treatments/pediatric-hydrocephalus/)* — CSF accumulation in the cerebral ventricles,
      causing ventricular enlargement and raised intracranial pressure. Treated with ventriculoperitoneal shunting or
      endoscopic third ventriculostomy.


      *[Tethered spinal cord](/treatments/tethered-spinal-cord/)* — Abnormal attachment of the spinal cord to
      surrounding structures prevents the cord from ascending normally during growth, causing progressive neurological
      dysfunction in the lower limbs, bladder, and bowel. Treated by surgical release.


      *[Craniosynostosis](/treatments/craniosynostosis/)* — Premature fusion of one or more cranial sutures prevents
      normal skull growth and may restrict brain development. Surgical remodelling before 12 months restores normal
      skull shape and relieves intracranial pressure.


      *Cerebral palsy* — A group of permanent, non-progressive motor disorders resulting from an injury to the
      developing brain. Neurosurgical management of spasticity includes intrathecal baclofen pump implantation and
      selective dorsal rhizotomy.


      **Principles of paediatric neurosurgical care**


      - Early intervention preserves neurological function: untreated raised intracranial pressure, cord tethering, and
      spasticity all cause progressive, often irreversible harm

      - Surgical technique must accommodate the small size and fragility of paediatric anatomy while allowing for future
      growth

      - Long-term developmental follow-up is an integral part of care — the impact of brain and spinal cord conditions
      extends far beyond the surgical result

      - Family education and support are essential components of management
