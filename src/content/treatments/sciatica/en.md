  - type: prose
    heading: "What sciatica is"
    body: |
      Sciatica is a symptom, not a diagnosis. It refers to pain that radiates along the sciatic nerve — the largest nerve in the body — which forms from the lower lumbar and upper sacral nerve roots and travels through the buttock and down the back of the leg.

      In most cases the pain is caused by a herniated disc compressing one of those nerve roots as it exits the spine. Less commonly, the compression comes from spinal stenosis, piriformis muscle spasm, or a mass along the nerve's path. Identifying which root and what is compressing it is the point of the first consultation.

  - type: prose
    heading: "How it presents"
    body: |
      The classic pattern is a sharp, electric-shock-like pain that shoots from the buttock down the back or side of one leg, sometimes reaching the foot. The pain often follows a specific dermatome — the strip of skin supplied by one nerve root — and knowing that strip lets the clinician predict which root is affected before imaging confirms it.

      Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the leg or foot changes the plan: those symptoms indicate the nerve is not just irritated but functionally impaired, and urgent evaluation is warranted if weakness is progressive.
