      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          An abnormal sideways (S- or C-shaped) curve of the spine, usually of unknown cause and noticed during
          adolescent growth.
      - label: Typical signs
        value: Uneven shoulders, one shoulder blade more prominent, uneven waist, one hip higher, a rib hump on forward bend.
      - label: How we treat it
        value: >-
          Observation for mild curves, bracing for moderate progressing curves in growing spines, surgery for severe or
          rapidly progressing curves.
  - type: prose
    heading: What scoliosis is
    body: >
      Scoliosis is a lateral (sideways) curvature of the spine that measures more than 10° on a specific X-ray
      measurement (the Cobb angle). Small curves are extremely common and often clinically silent; the ones that need
      active management are the ones that grow.


      In most cases (~80%) the cause is unknown — adolescent idiopathic scoliosis. In others, scoliosis is a feature of
      a syndrome, neuromuscular disease, or a congenital vertebral anomaly. The distinction changes the workup and the
      risk of progression.
  - type: panels
    heading: Adolescent vs adult scoliosis
    intro: >-
      Two very different clinical problems share the same name. Deciding which one you have is the first job at the
      consultation.
    panels:
      - eyebrow: Type A
        title: Adolescent idiopathic scoliosis
        items:
          - Discovered during growth (10–18 years old).
          - Risk factor is remaining growth — hence bracing while the skeleton is still immature.
          - Cosmetic and postural concerns dominate; pain is unusual until the curve is severe.
      - eyebrow: Type B
        title: Adult / degenerative scoliosis
        items:
          - Develops later from degenerative disc and facet changes.
          - Pain, radiculopathy, and stenosis dominate; the curve is often incidental.
          - Treatment targets the symptoms, not the curve number.
  - type: stats
    heading: What we see in clinic
    items:
      - value: 80%
        label: Of adolescent scoliosis is idiopathic (no identifiable cause)
      - value: 10°
        label: Cobb angle threshold — below this is not called scoliosis
      - value: 45–50°
        label: Typical threshold for surgical discussion in adolescents
  - type: facts
    items:
      - >-
        The forward-bend test (Adam's test) is the standard screening — a visible rib hump on forward bend is the key
        sign.
      - X-ray is the definitive investigation, MRI reserved for atypical curves or neurological signs.
      - Bracing works only during skeletal growth — once growth is complete, it does not correct the curve.
      - Curves under 20° in a skeletally mature patient rarely progress and usually just need periodic review.
      - Modern surgical techniques give substantial correction with much shorter recovery than 20 years ago.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: >-
      Care is matched to the curve severity, skeletal maturity, and the pattern of progression. Most curves land in
      observation.
    note: >-
      Progression is judged by comparing X-rays over time, not by a single snapshot. Skipping the follow-up is what
      turns a small curve into a large one.
    panels:
      - title: Observation
        subtitle: Mild curves
        items:
          - X-ray every 6–12 months during growth
          - Adam's test at each visit
          - Escalate only if the curve progresses
      - title: Bracing
        subtitle: Moderate curves, growing skeleton
        items:
          - Rigid custom brace (TLSO, Boston, Milwaukee)
          - Worn 16–23 hours per day while skeletally immature
          - "Purpose: prevent progression, not correct the curve"
      - title: Physiotherapy
        subtitle: Adjunctive at every stage
        items:
          - Postural training
          - Core and paraspinal strengthening
          - Schroth-method exercises where available
      - title: Surgery
        subtitle: Severe or progressive curves
        items:
          - Posterior spinal fusion with instrumentation
          - Anterior spinal fusion in selected curves
          - Growing rod systems for very young patients
          - Vertebral body tethering for suitable adolescents
