    heading: "What spasmodic torticollis is"
    body: |
      Cervical dystonia — also called spasmodic torticollis — is a painful condition in which neck muscles contract involuntarily, causing the head to twist or turn to one side, or to tilt forward or backward uncontrollably.

      It is a rare disorder that can occur at any age, but most often begins in middle-aged people and affects women more than men. Symptoms generally develop gradually and then plateau rather than continuously worsen.

      **Directions of abnormal head posture:**
      - Chin pulled toward the shoulder (most common)
      - Ear tilted toward the shoulder
      - Chin tilted upward
      - Chin tilted downward

      Some people experience combinations of postures, along with a jerking head motion. Neck pain radiating into the shoulders is common, as are headaches. In some patients, pain is exhausting and disabling.

      **Causes:** In most cases the cause is unknown. A family history of the disorder is sometimes present, and gene mutations associated with cervical dystonia have been identified. Head, neck, or shoulder injuries are also linked to onset.
