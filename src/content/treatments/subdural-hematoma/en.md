  - type: prose
    heading: "What a subdural hematoma is"
    body: |
      A subdural hematoma is a type of intracranial hematoma — a collection of blood that forms beneath the skull and beneath the dura mater (the tough membrane surrounding the brain) but outside the brain tissue itself. It typically results from a tear in the veins passing between the brain and the dura, or from a laceration on the brain itself. A skull fracture may or may not be present.

      **Symptoms** vary with injury severity. Moderate to severe head injury requires immediate medical attention:
      - Severe headache
      - Confusion or disorientation
      - Loss of consciousness
      - Blurred vision
      - Vomiting
      - Short-term memory loss
      - Slurred speech, difficulty walking, or dizziness
      - Weakness or numbness on one side of the body
      - Seizures or behaviour changes
      - One pupil appearing larger than the other

      **Raised intracranial pressure:** Head injury may cause brain swelling. Because the skull has limited space, swelling raises pressure inside the skull (intracranial pressure, ICP) — which can lead to brain damage. Severe cases require ICP monitoring.

      **Surgical option:** When the hematoma is significant and does not resolve, burr hole evacuation (surgical drainage) may be required.
