      - label: What it is
        value: >-
          Neck injury from sudden backward or forward head movement (acceleration–deceleration), classically from motor
          vehicle collision. Soft-tissue injury to ligaments and muscles.
      - label: Typical signs
        value: Neck pain and stiffness (may be delayed hours to days), headache, shoulder pain, numbness or tingling in arms.
      - label: How we treat it
        value: >-
          Acute: immobilisation, analgesia, muscle relaxants. Early mobilisation once acute pain settles. Chronic:
          physiotherapy, cognitive behavioural therapy, and — rarely — interventional pain procedures.
  - type: prose
    heading: What whiplash is
    body: >
      Whiplash occurs when the neck is forcibly flexed and extended (or vice versa) by sudden acceleration or
      deceleration, classically in motor vehicle collisions. The underlying injury is to the soft tissues — ligaments,
      muscles, and facet joints — though MRI and X-ray are often normal.
  - type: stats
    heading: What we see in clinic
    items:
      - value: 10–15%
        label: Develop chronic whiplash-associated disorder (WAD)
      - value: 3–7 days
        label: Average onset of peak pain
      - value: 80%
        label: Recover within 3 months with early mobilisation
  - type: facts
    items:
      - >-
        Severity of initial injury does not predict chronicity — some rear-end collisions at low speed cause chronic
        pain.
      - Psychological factors (catastrophising, fear-avoidance) are independent predictors of chronicity.
      - >-
        Imaging (X-ray, MRI, CT) is often normal despite significant symptoms; absence of imaging findings does not
        invalidate the diagnosis.
  - type: panels
    heading: How it is treated
    intro: Early mobilisation and active rehabilitation are key. Most recover with conservative care.
    panels:
      - title: Acute management
        subtitle: First 1–2 weeks
        items:
          - Soft cervical collar for brief immobilisation (< 3 days) if needed
          - Paracetamol and NSAIDs for pain
          - Muscle relaxants if spasm is limiting
      - title: Early rehabilitation
        subtitle: Weeks 1–4
        items:
          - Gentle neck mobilisation and range-of-motion exercises
          - Progressive strengthening of neck and shoulder muscles
          - Posture education and ergonomic advice
      - title: Chronic pain management
        subtitle: If pain persists > 3 months
        items:
          - Intensive physiotherapy with an emphasis on fear-avoidance reduction
          - Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
          - Facet joint injections or medial branch blocks if focal pain suggests facet origin
      - title: Psychological support
        subtitle: Important for catastrophising and fear-avoidance
        items:
          - Reassurance and education about expected recovery
          - Return-to-work and activity graded exposure
